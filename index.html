    History

    1. 2009

      The back room

      Chris Williams was a picture framer on Marlow Yard when he talked the owner of the shop into lending him its back room for a month. The first show was eleven small oils by friends, hung on nails borrowed from the workshop. Forty people came. Three paintings sold, two of them to his mother.

    2. 2010

      One artist at a time

      After a year of crowded evenings, Chris set the rule the gallery still keeps: one artist per show, no group exhibitions, and the walls repainted between every hang. That spring Jack Wolfstein turned up with canvases wrapped in bin bags, having carried them across London on the 55 bus.

    3. 2011

      Pompeii

      Wolfstein’s first solo show, Last Day of Pompeii, sold out in eight days. A short review in a local paper did more than anyone expected, and the framing shop’s owner, tired of the queue at his door, offered the whole ground floor.

    4. 2012

      The ground floor

      The renovation took four months and most of the savings. The concrete floor stayed as it was, stained with glue and paint. Lights were planned around the two north windows. The gallery opened Wednesday to Sunday so there would always be two quiet days to hang the next show.

    5. 2013

      Longer walls

      Mira Lindqvist arrived from Gothenburg with ceremonial scenes so crowded they needed more wall than the room had. For the first time the gallery built temporary walls, and visitors walked a corridor of processions and gatherings to reach the back.

    6. 2014

      The reading table

      Every show began to come with a printed sheet written by the artist, not by a curator. The sheets are free and sit in a wooden box by the door. Twelve years on, the archive of them is the closest thing the gallery has to a catalogue.

    7. 2015

      Restorations

      Tobias Kern showed oil paintings made over old archive photographs, part restorer and part forger. A museum in Rotterdam borrowed three of the works, the first time anything left Marlow Yard on loan rather than on sale.

    8. 2016

      The difficult year

      The rent doubled in a single letter. For a few weeks it looked as though the gallery would close. Instead, every artist it had shown gave one work to a sale on a Saturday in November. By Sunday evening it had covered two years of rent.

    9. 2017

      Upstairs

      With the lease secured for ten years, the empty first floor became a studio. Residencies run for three months, cost nothing, and come with one condition: at the end, show what you made, finished or not.

    10. 2018

      Ordinary places

      Amara Osei spent her residency collecting civic photographs from the borough’s archive and reassembling them into large, quiet compositions. People from the neighbourhood came in looking for their own streets, and often found them.

    11. 2019

      The back wall

      River Cole painted straight onto the gallery’s rear wall. When the show closed the wall was painted over, as agreed from the start. Photographs are the only record. On the last day the queue reached the end of the yard.

    12. 2020

      Postcards

      The gallery was closed for five months. Instead of an online viewing room it sent postcards: every week, one printed work mailed to anyone who asked for it. By the autumn the list had four thousand addresses.

    13. 2021

      Slower

      Ines Calder, a botanist before she was a painter, brought the patience of the specimen table with her. Her show changed the calendar: exhibitions now run for ten weeks instead of six, so a painting can be visited more than once.

    14. 2022

      The studio as subject

      Sam Ferreira showed no finished canvases at all, only tools, rags and residue from two years of work. It was the most argued-about show in the gallery’s history, and the most visited.

    15. 2024

      The field

      The gallery went online the way it works offline. There is no search bar and no filters, only a field of works you wander through, the way you would wander through the room. Every piece is still hung by hand, one artist at a time.

    16. 2026

      Today

      Seven artists, one studio upstairs and the same concrete floor. The reading table is still by the door. The walls are still repainted between shows. The next one is being hung on a Monday.

    The room is open Wednesday to Sunday. The field is open always.
